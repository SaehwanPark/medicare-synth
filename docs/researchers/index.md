# For researchers

Medicare-Synth helps you explore ideas with artificial, Medicare-like datasets
before working with restricted Medicare files. It is a research preparation and
methods-testing sandbox—not a source of Medicare population estimates.

## Choose your goal

### Explore available data

Learn how beneficiary, enrollment, condition, and claims-style records relate,
and what one row represents versus a patient count.

→ [Understanding the data](understanding-the-data/) ·
[What you can do](what-you-can-do/)

### Plan an analysis

Walk the supported 2021 Original Medicare FFS-like diabetes and any-inpatient
example: what fields exist, which public statistics were used as anchors, and
what you can practice in code before restricted access.

→ [Diabetes–hospitalization example](diabetes-hospitalization-example/) ·
[Getting started](getting-started/)

### Compare assumptions

See how synthetic datasets can match the same selected published rates yet
differ on free assumptions (for example background inpatient risk). Use that to
stress-test analysis code—not to recover “the” Medicare answer.

→ [Interpreting results](interpreting-results/) ·
[Limitations](limitations/)

## Before you go further

<div class="callout callout-warning">
  <div class="callout-title">Not Medicare findings</div>
  Do not cite generated associations, model performance, or calibrated rates as empirical results about Medicare beneficiaries. Structural checks and agreement with selected published statistics do not authorize population inference.
</div>
